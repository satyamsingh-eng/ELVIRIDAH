Elvaridah × KARAX / Meeting Intelligence / Proposed workflow

Elvaridah’s client workcontinues after the meeting.

Client meetings create the commitments a service team must deliver. After the call, someone still has to record decisions, assign owners and dates, prepare the follow-up, and update the working systems. This proposed KARAX workflow prepares that handover for review.

01 / 12
The operating problem

The work after the meeting is part of delivery.

Without a structured handover, follow-up is reconstructed from notes, memory, and messages. That creates delay, repeated clarification, and dependence on the person who attended the call.

Evidence boundaryElvaridah meeting telemetry and client feedback are not verified. This page shows a proposed workflow, an illustrative meeting, and clearly labelled modelled values.

What the research means for the person doing this work each week:

Before the meetingRebuild context from last week’s thread. Draft the agenda. Search old notes for what was promised.
During the meetingAttention divides between listening and note-taking. Decisions and owners are partly reconstructed from memory afterwards.
After the meetingRewrite rough notes. Chase people for owners and dates. Draft follow-up. Update the tracker by hand.
Weeks laterSearch messages and notebooks to confirm what was agreed — the record never becomes easier to use.

12 / Validation

This page becomes a results case study only after Elvaridah confirms the baseline and reviews the output.

Proposed validation

Run 5–10 comparable client meetings.

Record the current time spent on notes, actions, follow-up, and tracker updates. Compare it with KARAX preparation plus human review.

The decision follows the evidence: continue, change the workflow, or stop. A client-approved outcome replaces the illustrative example.

Success checks

What the client should be able to verify

  1. Time from meeting end to usable notes
  2. Time from meeting end to approved follow-up
  3. Actions captured with the right owners and dates
  4. Actions still open after seven days
  5. Human review time and edits required

Client feedback and decision points:

Week 1 · TrustThe team compares KARAX notes with its own records. Accuracy of decisions, owners, and dates determines whether the pilot continues.
Week 2 · UsabilityShort interviews capture what worked, what felt slow, and where human judgement is still required.
Weeks 3–4 · MeasurementThe success checks above are measured on real meetings. The illustrative 5h 28m model is replaced by client data.
Week 4 · DecisionContinue, change, or stop. Approved quotes, screenshots, and metrics become the evidence layer only with client approval.

Next step: validate the workflow on real meetings.

The proposed path is capture → prepared record → human approval → approved update. The pilot tests whether it reduces follow-up effort, improves ownership, and preserves control.
